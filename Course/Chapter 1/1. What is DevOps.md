# What is DevOps?

DevOps is a combination of cultural philosophies, practices, and tools that brings software development and IT operations together. These practices enable organizations to deliver new application features and improved services to customers at a higher velocity.

## DevOps model defined

DevOps combines cultural philosophies, practices, and tools to increase an organization's ability to deliver applications and services at high velocity. It helps teams evolve and improve products faster than organizations using traditional software development and infrastructure management processes. This speed enables organizations to better serve their customers and compete more effectively in the market.

## DevOps topologies

Here are several examples of DevOps topologies and anti-patterns (anti-types):

1. Developer and operations silos: This is a common topology where development and operations are separate entities that pass work from one side to the other.
2. Developer and operations collaboration: This model balances collaboration between development and operations, allowing each team to specialize while still working together effectively.
3. DevOps team silo: This anti-pattern creates a separate DevOps team between development and operations, which can further isolate the two groups instead of integrating them.
4. Fully shared and integrated operations responsibilities: In this topology, there is little or no separation between development and operations. Success depends on shared purpose, collaboration, and involvement across all processes.

For more information on other DevOps topologies, see: https://web.devopstopologies.com/

## Two-pizza teams

As organizations increased the number of services and built more complex architectures, Amazon needed better efficiency and a stronger DevOps model. The company reorganized its functional hierarchy into smaller, more agile, autonomous teams, commonly referred to as "two-pizza teams," which worked on microservices. This approach improved agility for both the teams and the applications they supported.

![Two-pizza teams](https://github.com/MinenhleNkosi/DevOps-Mastery/blob/main/Course/Chapter%201/images/image2.png)

Two-pizza teams typically include 6–8 members who work on a single project using agile principles. Each team is closely aligned with the success of its own products or applications. Team members bring complementary skills and ownership across the development lifecycle, including testing, security, and performance. The team is responsible for the full development pipeline, including testing, security, and monitoring.
